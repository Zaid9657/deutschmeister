The reasons for moving material are:

- **General comparison rules → A2.1:** A1 needs common comparisons and preferences, while comprehensive formation—including productive umlaut patterns and attributive superlatives—is unnecessary for completing Start Deutsch 1.
- **General Konjunktiv II → A2.1:** A1 needs usable polite expressions, and Goethe explicitly permits these to be learned lexically rather than as a conjugational system.

Risks:

- **Notice coverage is not systematic teaching.** Each addition needs receptive work, typed retrieval, communicative use and delayed review; adding forms to a function-word allowlist does not meet M7.
- **L12 remains crowded.** Teach its practice in three passes—existing *haben*-Perfekt, participles without *ge-*, then auxiliary choice—and revisit them after the lesson; a single four-item exercise is insufficient.
- **The implementation has stricter limits than wording alone.** Dialogues allow only 6–10 lines and two speakers, so the proposals require replacements and redistribution, not wholesale appending; preserve vocabulary coverage, existing grammatical examples, translations and audio references.
- **The practice routing currently follows existing grammar slugs.** New secondary learning goals need actual exercises and review routing; the twelve-slot cache must not determine the pedagogical ceiling.
- **Do not describe textbook variation as examination certainty.** The evidence rejects both “all missing items belong in A2” and “every heading in the internal standard requires a complete A1 paradigm”; the appropriate scope is the bounded repertoire decided above.
