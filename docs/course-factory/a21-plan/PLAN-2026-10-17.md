parallel. This file is the A2.1 half. A second agent plans A2.2 and a third measures data and engine
gaps. Building starts after A1.2 ships. This plan changes no code, data, migration or configuration.

### 1.2 Method

1. **Engine.** I read the A1.1 and A1.2 data and the engine paths that CONTRACT.md names. §5 cites each
   one by file and line.
2. **Official PDFs.** The goethe.de and Hueber PDFs reach the fetcher as compressed binaries, three of
   them encrypted. I extracted their text locally with a small reader that has no dependencies
   (FlateDecode, object streams, ToUnicode maps, and the standard security handler with the empty user
   password). Then I read the inventory pages myself. No PDF content goes into the repository.
3. **The boundary.** It comes from the A2.2 plan at the coordinator's request (§2.2). My own rule was:
   the Goethe A2 inventory (S1) lists the point, and at least one of the two Lehrwerke (S4, S5) teaches
   it in A2.1, or the A1.2 M7 decision handed it over. That rule disagrees with the adopted split in
   four places, and §2.4 states each one. §2.5 shows what changes if the owner picks the other split.
4. **Measurements** (§7 has the commands and the outputs):
   - the legacy bank items each topic supplies (`filterPool` at level `a2.1`);
   - the Goethe A2 headwords not yet known at the end of A1.2, using the validator's own
     `levelLexicon('a1.2')`;
   - a first-pass Wortfeld per Lektion, checked against the A1 Wortfelder and against S2;
   - the facts the course already states about each character (`PERSONAS_A11/A12`);
   - the level-specific assumptions in the engine.
5. **Copyright.** Can-dos and inventories are paraphrased, and no word list or descriptor table is
   reproduced. The only quotation is the short phrase from S2 p. 4 in the source table. All German
   example sentences are my own.

### 1.3 The target exam in brief

- **Lesen** (30 min, 4 Teile × 5 items):
  - T1: a media text, multiple choice with three options;
  - T2: information boards and programmes, three-option MC;
  - T3: correspondence, three-option MC;
  - T4: ads, matching.
- **Hören** (30 min, 4 Teile × 5 items):
  - T1: radio, answering machine and announcements, three-option MC;
  - T2: one connected conversation, matching;
  - T3: five short conversations, MC with pictures;
  - T4: a radio interview, Ja/Nein (the overview table calls it Richtig/Falsch).
- **Schreiben** (30 min):
  - T1: an SMS, a personal message to keep in touch, 20–30 words, three points;
  - T2: an E-Mail, a semi-formal message to arrange something, 30–40 words, three points.
- **Sprechen** (≈15 min for two candidates):
  - T1: four question cards on personal information, answered with a partner;
  - T2: talking about one's own life from a card with four prompts;
  - T3: planning an activity together from two calendars.
- **Scoring:**
  - 25 points per part, 60/100 to pass;
  - the written parts (Lesen, Hören, Schreiben) need ≥ 45/75 and Sprechen ≥ 15/25.
- **Writing assessment:**
  - task fulfilment per point, plus register;
  - then range and control of coherence, vocabulary and structures;
  - a text under 50 % of the word count (10 words in Teil 1, 15 in Teil 2) scores E, which makes the
    whole task 0.

Sources: S3 pp. 2–3, 21–27, 37, 42.

**What "bounded" means for weil, dass and wenn** (rows A8/A10 of the A2.2 plan):
- Introduced: the Nebensatz **after** the main clause, with **one finite verb** at the end („…, weil
  die Straße nass war.“).
- Left to A2.2's `subordinating-conjunctions` (L4) and `subordinate-word-order` (L8): the Nebensatz
  first with the inverted main clause, and a verb cluster at the end (modal + infinitive, Perfekt,
  rejoined separable verb).

The horizon enforces this (§5.2): a weil, dass or wenn clause with a cluster is unservable in A2.1.

**What "bounded" means for könnte and sollte:** two chunks for advice and suggestions, *du solltest …*
and *wir könnten …*, with the infinitive at the end. No hätte, wäre or würde paradigm: that is A2.2 L11.

**D-3 · weil/dass/wenn arrive in A2.1 with only half their syntax.**

## 4. The four checkpoints and the final test

### 4.1 Composition

The engine shape stays the same: 20 items in five sections, 70 % from the chapter and 30 % from
earlier chapters, unlimited attempts and remediation sets (`buildCheckpoint.js:1–29`, a12 branch). What
changes at A2 is the content of the sections. **Each Goethe A2 Lesen and Hören Teil is mirrored in
exactly one checkpoint**, so the four papers together rehearse the whole written exam once. Sprechen
stays in the Lektionen (12 open tasks); in the checkpoints it remains the scored read-aloud.

- **Writing.** The four checkpoint tasks are transfer tasks with their own situations, outside

its slug, and the introducing Lektion is read from `primarySlug`, as `constructions.js` requires. Each
one goes into `OFF_LIMITS_FORMS_A21`.

**Bounded points that have no A2.1 slug** (Genitiv-s, dass, sollte/könnte, weil, the dative -en, wenn)
need a per-level map from the form to its Lektion. Today the table is keyed by slug, and a slug is
primary only once. The A1.2 row already does exactly this: it files `denn` under L6's slug and `war/hatte`
under L7's (`validate-curriculum.mjs:714–721`). A2.1 reuses that trick, so the bounded point is filed
under its Lektion's primary slug.
