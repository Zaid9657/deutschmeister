### The three slip classes from the Lektion 2–3 rollout (`check.js`, `tests/checker-slips.test.mjs`)

First, what a TYPO is worth: it counts as **correct** in the lesson (`PracticeItem`: `correct =
result !== WRONG`), the checkpoint (`isItemCorrect`) and the review (`reviewGrading`); it only adds
the offer to type it again. So every slip rule is a rule about what scores, and each is pinned on what
must stay wrong.

- **Arabic-Indic and Persian digits** (`westernDigits`, first step of the fold, every item kind, and
  `tagError`): „٨ Uhr“ = „8 Uhr“, a dictated phone number typed „٠٤٢ ٣٣٨١“ passes; another number
  in Arabic digits stays wrong.
- **Two neighbouring letters swapped** (`oneSwap`) in a word of five letters or more is a TYPO
  („Eltren“, „Brduer“, „gebroen“, „mdüe“). Never two vowels („speile“, „Meite“: ie/ei is spelling the
  learner must know and often another word; „Huete“ folds to „Hüte“). The ending rule still holds for
  a swap that moves the last letter, except in a noun inside a sentence („Abfahtr“, „Rucksakc“), where
  a swap changes no letter and so no ending. „sei“ for „sie“ and „dcih“ stay wrong (short grammar words).
- **A slip in a word of three or four letters**: a narrow rule. Only a lower-case content word
  (adjective, verb, adverb), only one consonant dropped from inside it („at“ for „alt“, „get“ for
  „geht“, „kom“ for „komm“) or two letters inside it swapped („jnug“), and neither form a grammar word.
  Measured first: of 143 such shapes over the course's short content words, 3 are themselves course
  words (habt→hat, arm→am, Mann→man), all grammar words, so all refused. Nouns are out because the
  blind corpus showed „Kid“ for „Kind“; vowel and last-letter drops („Bs“, „gu“, „Ur“) change the
  word. Articles, pronouns, sein/haben forms and prepositions stay wrong at any slip („di“, „is“, „bi“,
  „un“, „su“), as the course standard says. „jugn“ (the rollout's example) is a last-letter swap in a
  four-letter word and stays wrong.

**Proof on the blind answers.** A new blind batch written by Codex for these classes
(`blind-corpus-2026-11-19/slips-1/`, 72 tasks: typed gaps, sentences, corrections and dictated lines
from both courses, 498 answers; graded by `grade-slips.mjs`, which reads a gap's word off the whole
sentence and skips slips in printed text): of the 342 gradable lines, answers the blind teacher
accepts but the checker rejected **108 → 55** (53 now accepted), mistakes accepted **9 → 9** (0 newly
accepted), correct answers newly rejected **0**. The 9 are older one-letter cases that make another
word („isst“ for „ist“ ×4, „Stahl“, „Mitte“, „laufen“, „Spruch“; a follow-up). On the committed
2,262-line corpus, old against new checker on the same items (`compare-checkers.mjs`, 1,773 gradable
lines): **0 changes** either way. The 55 still rejected: two-vowel swaps (8), slips in grammar words
(the standard), vowel or last-letter drops, and neighbouring-key substitutions in short words.

**What can move a final-check result** (`src/lib/lesson/finalCheck.js` on the rollout branch builds
on `checkAnswer`): (1) a typed turn with Arabic-Indic or Persian digits outside a `listen` turn now
matches its digits (the `listen` turn already folded them); (2) a typed turn with two neighbouring
letters swapped in a word of five letters or more, or a short-word slip of the two shapes above, now
grades TYPO instead of WRONG, so the turn passes without the second try (and `typedResult`'s TYPO
rank applies). Nothing that passed before can fail now.
