# Ankommo.de — competitive analysis

**Written 2026-09-03.** Requested by the owner: what Ankommo does, how it is
built, how it makes money, how much, and what a materially better version
looks like.

## How this was researched, and what that limits

`ankommo.de`, `ankommo.com`, `learn.ankommo.de` and `app.gogermany.me` are all
**blocked by this environment's egress proxy**, so no page was fetched or read
directly. Every fact below comes from one of three sources, and each claim says
which:

- **[SERP]** — the search index's own title/description/snippet for a named
  Ankommo URL. These are the site's real words, but truncated and possibly
  weeks stale.
- **[DFS]** — DataForSEO, live, 2026-09-03: `domain_rank_overview` and
  `ranked_keywords`, Germany (location 2276), German and English.
- **[SOCIAL]** — public profile counts and post text from the search index.

Anything marked **[EST]** is my estimate with the method stated. No revenue
figure here is measured. Ankommo publishes no revenue and I could not reach its
checkout.

## 1. What Ankommo actually is

The one-line self-description: *"Everything You Need to Build a Life in
Germany — from your first German lesson to your permanent residence date:
courses, guides and free calculators for visas, salary, Anmeldung and
citizenship timing."* [SERP, homepage]

**It is not a German-learning site.** It is a *relocation and settlement*
site for internationals in (or moving to) Germany, in which a German course is
one module among many. That distinction is the whole analysis.

Operator: **Abdullah Tayyab**, a private individual at a Berlin address
(10437, Prenzlauer Berg), contact `hello@ankommo.de` [SERP, /privacy]. LinkedIn
lists him as an IT project manager (PMP, AWS, LMU Munich) with Ankommo as his
own venture [SERP]. The project was previously branded **GoGermany**
(`app.gogermany.me` still resolves to Ankommo copy) [SERP]. There is a
Crunchbase company page [SERP]. This is a **solo operator**, not a company.

Its own Terms are careful and worth noting: *"Ankommo is a self-study German
language learning tool… not a language school, not a certified training
provider"* and *"makes no guarantee of any language examination result,
certification, visa outcome, employment…"* [SERP, /terms]. Same defensive
posture we hold.

### How it is built

I could not fetch a byte of HTML, so I will not guess a framework. What the URL
structure *does* show: clean flat routes, an `app.` and a `learn.` subdomain
splitting the interactive product from the marketing site, and a former
`.me` domain still serving. The public estate is content-and-calculator heavy —
a large number of static, individually-indexable pages (the Einbürgerungstest
alone is 300+ question URLs), with the logged-in course behind `learn.`/`app.`.
That is the same two-front-end split we run, arrived at independently.

## 2. How it makes money

Five stacked layers, cheapest to most expensive. All prices [SERP].

| Layer | Product | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Chapters 1–5 of every level, first mock set, all calculators | €0 |
| Self-serve sub | **Ankommo Pro** — 7 days free, then | **€4.99/month** |
| Self-serve one-off | **Course Pass**, per level | **€9.99 one-time** |
| Digital product | **Study Application Kit**, 10 chapters (ch. 1 free) | **€29** |
| Service | Visa Document Pre-Check | €79 |
| Service | Germany Job Route Audit (written, 2 working days) | €99 |
| Service | Written review of your real PR date | €99 |
| Service | Freelance/business registration, 60-min call | €149 |
| Service | Career support band (CV, cover letter, interview, 4-week job sprint) | **€49–€449** |
| Service | **Full Admission Support** ("Admission Sprint"), one intake | **€599** |

Plus a sixth layer he does not label: **affiliate**. `/fintiba-vs-expatrio`,
`/blocked-account-germany`, `/health-insurance-germany`, `/job-hunt-tools`,
`/free-certificates` and `/remote-jobs-europe` are textbook affiliate real
estate — comparison pages for blocked-account providers, insurers and job
boards, all of which run referral programmes. I found **no affiliate disclosure
page** in the index; that is a claim about absence from a search index, not
proof of absence.

**The shape of it:** the €4.99 subscription is not the business. It is the
cheapest possible tripwire that turns a follower into a paying customer. The
money is in the €79–€599 services and, probably, the affiliate pages.

## 3. How much he makes — estimate, not measurement

### The one number that matters: his traffic is not organic

| Ranked keywords (DE/English) | 3 | — |

| First backlink seen | **2026-08-20** | — |

Every one of the 120 keywords is flagged `is_new`. The domain has **one
referring domain** and its first backlink was seen two weeks ago. This site is
**brand new** and has essentially **no search presence**: its ranked positions
are almost all 60–100, i.e. pages 6–10, which convert to near-zero real clicks.
The 202 ETV is a model output and is generous.

Total addressable volume on just those 40 keywords: **87,390/month**. He is
positioned against it and currently capturing almost none of it.

### The actual engine: an audience he already had

[SOCIAL] **Instagram @abdidoesstuff: 107,000 followers.** TikTok
@abdidoesstuff: **29,700 followers, 658,900 likes.** An @ankommo brand account
("Join our 1000+ success stories"). Active Facebook distribution — individual
posts reaching 2,400+ reactions, and his comments appearing under large German
pages (DAAD, The Local Germany). Reels regularly draw thousands of likes with
`ankommo.de/citizenship-timeline` and `learn.ankommo.de` in the caption.

**This is the whole business.** Ankommo is a monetization surface bolted onto a
creator audience that already existed. The website is the checkout, not the
funnel.

### The estimate [EST]

Method: social-driven sessions × published price points × conventional
conversion bands. Stated so you can disagree with any input.

- **Sessions.** A 107K IG + 30K TikTok creator posting link-in-bio CTAs several
  times a week typically moves 2–8% of followers to a link over a month, very
  unevenly (one viral reel dominates). → **3,000–10,000 sessions/month**, of
  which organic search contributes ~200.
- **Pro €4.99 + Course Pass €9.99.** Self-serve conversion on a cold social
  visitor at this price: 0.5–2%. → 20–150 paying → **€150–€900/mo**. A 7-day
  free trial at €4.99 also implies meaningful trial churn.
- **Study Application Kit €29.** 5–25 sales → **€150–€725/mo**.
- **Services €79–€599.** The swing factor, and pure labour. A creator with this
  reach plausibly books 2–10 paid engagements a month at an average around
  €150–200 → **€300–€2,000/mo**.
- **Affiliate.** Blocked-account and insurance referrals pay roughly €20–60
  each. 5–20 conversions → **€100–€1,200/mo**.

**Estimated gross: roughly €700–€4,800/month, most plausibly low four
figures.** Confidence: low. The domain is weeks old, so there is no track
record to reason from, and a genuinely viral month could double the top of that
band while a quiet one could put it near zero. The "1000+ success stories"
figure is his own marketing copy, not a verified customer count.

**The important structural fact:** the majority of that estimate is *his own
time* (services), not product. If he stops working, most of it stops. His
software revenue — the €4.99 and €9.99 lines — is probably in the **low
hundreds per month**. That is the honest comparison point, and it is not far
above our own 9 live subscriptions.

## 4. What he does better than us

1. **He sells the destination; we sell the subject.** His promise is "build a
   life in Germany." German is chapter one of a journey that ends at a passport.
   Our promise is "learn German / pass telc B1." His framing owns the whole
   customer lifetime and every adjacent wallet; ours owns one exam.
2. **Free tools are a better acquisition asset than free articles.** A salary
   calculator is used, bookmarked, screenshotted and shared. Fifteen of them
   sit on keywords worth 87,000+ searches/month. Our best keyword target
   ("brief schreiben b1") is 1,900. **He is fishing in a pond 40× bigger.**
3. **He is English-first.** His buyers search in English; our entire public
   estate is in German. We have locked ourselves out of the way our own
   customers actually search before they arrive.
4. **He built distribution before he built product.** 107K followers is not a
   marketing channel, it is the asset. We built an excellent product and have
   ~1,554 registered users and no audience.
5. **A real price ladder, €4.99 → €599.** Multiple doors at multiple
   commitment levels. We have one €9.99 door and one €89 door.
6. **Nationality-specific landers** matched to a language he actually speaks
   (Urdu). High intent, near-zero competition.
7. **He monetizes attention immediately** with services, without waiting for
   product-market fit on software.

## 5. What he does worse — our openings

1. **The German product looks thin.** €4.99/month and €9.99/level is what you
   charge for a course you did not spend long building. Nothing in his indexed
   copy mentions AI speaking practice, AI writing feedback, sentence-level
   grammatical analysis, spaced repetition or real exam audio. **We have all
   five, and they are hard to build.** On product depth we are not behind him —
   we are well ahead.
2. **His mock exams are a paywall tease** — first set free, the rest behind a
   €9.99 pass [SERP, /german-mock-exams]. Ours are free, on real rails, with
   real audio, across four exams.
3. **Sprawl.** `/jobs-in-norway`, `/jobs-in-finland`, `/jobs-in-netherlands`,
   `/jobs-in-switzerland` are not "life in Germany." That is content farming,
   it is thin, and it is the sort of estate Google prunes.
4. **The revenue does not scale.** Services are his time. A €599 admission
   sprint is a week of work. There is no version of that reaching €10k/month
   without him hiring.
5. **No search moat at all.** One referring domain. Positions 60–100. If we
   build better tools on the same keywords, there is nothing stopping us
   outranking him — he has no authority to defend with.
6. **Legal exposure we deliberately do not carry.** He is a named private
   individual at a Berlin address, charging money for "visa route audits" and
   "visa document pre-checks" and consultations on citizenship requirements.
   Paid immigration advice in Germany is restricted under the
   Rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz, and he is personally, locally liable for it.
   **This is precisely the German-law entanglement you have said the business
   must stay out of.** It is the single strongest reason not to copy the
   services half of his model.

## 6. How to build a much better version

The instinct to copy him is half right. Copy the **mechanics**; do not copy the
**business**.

### The thing to internalise first

His website is not why he earns. **His audience is.** He has a mediocre product
and a great channel; we have a strong product and no channel. If we clone his
site and keep our distribution, we get his revenue divided by a hundred.
The transferable lesson is not "add calculators" — it is **he built the
audience before the product, and we did it in the opposite order.**

### Take these three things

**A. A free-tool acquisition layer — but on tools only we can build.**
His tools are money tools (salary, tax) that anyone could clone; he wins them
on volume, not on right-to-win. Ours should be **exam and status tools**, where
our exam engine is the moat and the volume is still large:

- an **Einbürgerungstest trainer** (his is the smartest thing on his site, the
  BAMF catalogue is public, the German-language volume is very large, and we
  already have exam rails, scoring and a question renderer);
- a **"which German exam do I actually need"** decider (visa/PR/citizenship →
  telc vs Goethe vs DTZ), which is the question that precedes every purchase we
  want to make;
- a **B1-für-Einbürgerung timeline** — his `/citizenship-timeline` is his most
  socially-shared page and it does not know anything about German exams; ours
  would;
- a **Modelltest scorer** that marks an official free paper on the real scale
  (we already documented the three marking systems in the Modelltest Leitfaden).

Each is a linkable, shareable, screenshot-able asset. Each ends at our product
rather than at an affiliate.

**B. Open the English (and beyond) door.** Our public estate is German-only.
The person searching "telc b1 exam preparation" in English, Turkish, Arabic or
Russian is our exact customer, three months before they can read our homepage.
This is a large, currently-zero traffic surface, and unlike his salary
calculator it is squarely inside our competence.

**C. A real price ladder.** One €9.99 door and one €89 door is not a ladder.
An entry price below the impulse threshold, a per-exam one-off, and something
substantial at the top is a shape worth arguing about — that argument is the
monetization conversation you asked to have, and it is still open.

### Leave these

- **The 1:1 services.** They are his best revenue line and they are wrong for
  us: they do not scale, they consume the founder, and the visa/immigration
  half carries exactly the German legal exposure the ownership structure exists
  to avoid.
- **The country-jobs content farm.** Cheap to write, thin, off-mission.
- **Undercharging the language product to €4.99.** We should not race a
  weaker product to the bottom.

### What "much better" concretely means

Same lifecycle framing as his — *first German word to permanent residence* —
but with **the exam as the spine instead of the errand list**, an actually deep
product behind it (speaking, writing feedback, X-Ray, SRS, real audio, four
mock exams), free tools that we uniquely can build, and no personal legal
surface in Germany. That is a defensible business. His is a good creator
funnel with a thin product attached.

**And the honest caveat on all of it:** none of the above solves the problem
that he has 107,000 followers and we have none. Whatever we decide to build,
the distribution question has to be answered alongside it, not after it.

## Open questions this raises for the monetization decision

1. Do we own the €13–50 "serious exam prep with real feedback" gap, or race
   downward toward his €4.99? (Still unanswered from 2026-09-02.)
2. Is an Einbürgerungstest trainer the next build? It is the highest-volume
   German-language surface adjacent to what we already have, and it converts
   into exactly our exam funnel.
3. Do we open an English-language public estate, and if so on which routes?
4. Does the €89 telc B1 course survive the model we choose? Nothing has sold;
   it is fully reopenable.

**Nothing further gets built on the money side until the model is agreed.**
