I would build **a Goethe B1 rehearsal kit for adults with an exam booked in the next eight weeks**, distributed through teachers and creators, with Telegram supporting daily practice.

The evidence supports testing this offer. Reaching €73k/month remains a hypothesis that needs proof of both willingness to pay and repeatable distribution.

**1. Choose a focused exam product with low delivery costs.**

Three genuinely different models deserve consideration. Scores run from 1 to 5; higher is better, including lower legal risk and lower AI costs. These are judgments under this founder’s constraints, not market measurements.

| Model | Revenue potential within 12 months | Time to first € | Founder workload | Legal risk | AI-cost risk |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Exam rehearsal kit:** €79–119 per buyer | 4 | 4: roughly 2–3 weeks, subject to legal review | 5: target 3 hours/week | 4 | 5 |
| **AI tutor subscription:** €29/month for conversation, correction and exam practice | 3 | 3: roughly 4–8 weeks | 3: roughly 4–6 hours/week | 1 | 2 |
| **Teacher software:** €299/school/month for task creation, marking drafts and classroom administration | 4 | 2: roughly 6–12 weeks | 2: roughly 6–10 hours/week initially | 3 | 4 |

The subscription requires thousands of paying subscribers while exam customers naturally leave after their exam. Teacher software has attractive account economics, but sales, onboarding and institutional support consume scarce founder time. Selling software to schools also requires careful review of the actual contractual and teaching arrangements.

**Pick the rehearsal kit.** It offers the fastest inexpensive test, bounded support and negligible AI inference costs during use.

The first buyer is an English-speaking adult who has completed B1 coursework, has booked Goethe B1 within two to eight weeks, and needs structured practice for writing and speaking. Start with existing users who meet those conditions, then reach similar learners through teachers serving migration cohorts.

Goethe B1 received **45 of 95 exam-specific responses**. That is the strongest initial signal, although the sample is small and acquisition-biased. Missing exam dates establish an information gap; they do not establish that everyone lacks urgency. Ask directly.

The €79 core product would contain:

- A 28-day practice calendar with daily sessions and longer rehearsal sessions.
- Twenty-four original writing tasks and eighteen speaking scenarios.
- Prerecorded speaking-partner turns, with pauses for the learner’s response.
- Fixed examples showing weaker and stronger responses, with annotated explanations.
- Six timed module rehearsals, printable checklists and downloadable audio.
- Ninety days of app access; downloaded materials remain usable.

Recordings stay on the learner’s device. Learners compare their work with examples themselves. Product support handles access, billing and errors in materials. A qualified German teacher reviews the complete content before publication; the physician founder supplies operational judgment, not claimed examination expertise.

Preserve statutory rights and implement the applicable electronic withdrawal function. Check the actual Lemon Squeezy journey, including confirmations. **6. Use thirty-day kill rules that change the business when evidence demands it.**

Agree on the following rules before launching:

- **Legal viability:** If the exact offer cannot launch lawfully within the available budget and operating constraints, stop this implementation. Preserve the revenue objective and redesign the delivery model.
- **Willingness to pay:** After 200 qualified offer views, including 100 new prospects, fewer than ten retained purchases is a failed offer test. Zero €79 purchases after at least 75 qualified full-price views rejects the proposed premium.
- **Product usefulness:** Among at least twenty buyers, fewer than 60% starting a rehearsal within 48 hours, or fewer than 30% returning for three sessions within seven days, triggers product revision before further acquisition spending.
- **Refunds and trust:** Refunds above 15% after twenty purchases, or repeated material errors in reviewed content, stop expansion until the cause is resolved.
- **Distribution:** After fifteen relevant partner conversations, zero attributable pilot commitments undermines the route to €73k/month. Organic traffic alone cannot carry the proposed scale plan.
- **Economics and workload:** Stop scaling any channel with acquisition cost above €25 per retained buyer once a meaningful paid sample exists. If support requires over ten founder minutes per buyer, redesign the product before adding volume.

If the traffic or buyer denominators are missing at day thirty, the experiment is incomplete. That is a distribution failure to resolve, rather than evidence that nobody will buy.

Continue investment only when customers pay the standard price, use the materials, and a channel can repeatedly bring similar buyers within the time and cost limits.
