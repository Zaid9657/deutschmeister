**1. Score: 2/5**

DeutschMeister has the beginnings of a useful exam-preparation workflow, but its commercial differentiation is unproven. Buyers can already obtain inexpensive simulations, AI writing feedback, AI speaking feedback, structured plans, or live teachers. The root cause is **choosing the wrong comparison category**: DeutschMeister compares itself with general language apps while proposing prices that compete with teacher-supported exam preparation. Its own exam package remains unavailable, and recorded writing usage is four users in 60 days—not evidence of a superior learning mechanism. (FACTS, “Activation & usage”; `src/data/competitorComparisons.js:85`; `comp__testgerman.md:373`.)

**Clean sheet: hybrid.** Reuse the existing exam tools, but sell a bounded diagnostic → practice → retest experience. Merely refreshing comparisons is cheap but commercially weak. Replacing the business with a paid diagnostic service is a real alternative, but its demand and delivery economics are unverified.

**The €149–€249 ambition crosses into the teacher-supported price band.**

The observed corridor is approximately **€20–€70 for self-study purchases**, around **€20/month for AI exam practice**, and **€89–€389 for supported preparation**. These are offer anchors, not measured willingness to pay.

At €149, DeutschMeister would cost 7.5 times telc-trainer’s promotional price and essentially the same as DeutschAkademie’s 12-hour course. At €249, it matches that school’s 24-hour offer. Those comparisons follow directly from the table.

Internally, three months of Pro cost €29.97; the captured €89 package chiefly adds sequencing to tools already included in Pro. Its own FAQ explains that relationship. Raising the price magnifies the need to demonstrate additional value. (`src/data/pricing.js:24,81`; `site__telc-b1-komplettvorbereitung.md`, “Was ist der Unterschied zum Pro-Abo?”)

**There is an opening, but it is the learning loop—not AI or the founder’s profession.**

Code on local main already provides three letters with revisions, ten guided speaking rehearsals, measured gap priorities, and a second short-test form. The second form has different items and listening references. This is materially stronger than the live page’s grammar-week description. ([Programme](/home/user/deutschmeister/src/data/programs/telcB1Komplett.js:50), `src/lib/telcPilot.js:48`, `tests/telc-pilot.test.mjs:96`; `site__telc-b1-komplettvorbereitung.md`, “Vier Wochen, ein Weg”.)

DeutschMeister’s potential advantages are therefore:

- Against general apps: a specific exam route, revised written work and targeted gap practice.
- Against exercise shops: an inspectable connection between diagnosis, correction and subsequent practice.
- Against live schools: practice without appointments, with lower delivery overhead.
- Against general AI tutors: persistent exam tasks and progress evidence.

These are **product-design advantages, not verified superiority**. Competitor accounts were not tested.

Two limitations prevent stronger claims. Writing uses a common four-by-five-point rubric; official-score equivalence is unverified. Speaking evaluation uses transcripts and explicitly cannot assess acoustic pronunciation, pauses or speaking speed. (`netlify/functions/evaluate-writing.mjs:293`; `netlify/functions/evaluate-speaking.mjs:82`.) The retest is a **Kurzversion**, not a full examination, and equal difficulty between forms is unverified. (`src/data/mockExams/telcB1.js:7`.)

**Wedge statement—proposed, [owner]:**

> “For learners near B1 with a booked telc exam: find your gaps, revise three letters, rehearse ten speaking tasks, and check your progress on different short-test questions.”

Defensibility would come from validated grading, accumulated outcome evidence and trusted distribution. Those assets are unverified today. Physician experience can support empathy; it does not establish examiner competence.
