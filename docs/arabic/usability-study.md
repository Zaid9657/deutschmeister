# Arabic edition — usability study protocol

Status: **protocol only.** Nobody has been recruited, contacted or tested. Recruiting and
running sessions is an owner decision (consent, compensation, data handling).

## Goal

Find out whether an Arabic-speaking adult with **no German and little English** can, without
help: understand what DeutschMeister offers, start A1.1, complete Lessons 1–3, understand the
feedback, keep their progress and come back the next day.

## Participants (5–8 is enough to find most problems)

- Native Arabic speakers, 18+, living in or moving to a German-speaking country.
- A0 German (no course taken; may know a few words).
- Little English: cannot comfortably read an English paragraph (self-report plus one short
  screening sentence).
- Mix of: dialect background (Levantine, Egyptian, Gulf, Maghrebi — the copy is Modern Standard
  Arabic, and Maghrebi readers are the likeliest to find it formal), age, phone vs. desktop,
  confidence with apps. At least two on a low-end Android phone.
- Not: DaF teachers, translators, people who work on the product.

## Setup

- Their own phone or computer, their own browser, no account, cookies as they choose.
- Start from a plain link to `https://deutsch-meister.de/ar/` (as it would arrive in Telegram).
- Moderator speaks Arabic, does **not** coach. Allowed: "What do you expect to happen?",
  "What are you looking for?". Not allowed: pointing, explaining the UI, translating.
- Screen + audio recording only with written consent; otherwise notes only.

## Tasks

1. "Look at this page. In your own words: what is it, and what does it cost?" (offer comprehension)
2. "Start learning German." (finds the start; does the app open in Arabic?)
3. "Do the first lesson." (no help; note every hesitation > 5 s)
4. During a wrong answer: "What did the app tell you?" (feedback comprehension)
5. When an audio item appears: "Can you hear it? What do you do if you can't?"
6. Lessons 2 and 3 (may be split across the session if fatigue shows).
7. "You want to continue tomorrow on your phone. What do you do?" (saving progress / account)
8. Next day, unmoderated: open the link again; then 5 recall items from Lesson 1–3 vocabulary
   and one sentence to build (Ich heiße …, Ich komme aus …).

## What to record per participant

| Measure | How |
|---|---|
| Hesitations | Count and location of pauses > 5 s; what they were looking at |
| Help-seeking | Opened `/ar/help/`? Asked the moderator? Switched language? |
| Wrong turns | Taps on something that did not do what they expected |
| Abandonment | Where, and their stated reason |
| Time | Per lesson, and to first correct answer |
| Comprehension | Task 1 and 4 answers scored: understood / partly / not |
| Next-day recall | 5 words + 1 sentence, scored per item |
| Words they did not understand | Exact Arabic terms they stumbled on (feeds the glossary) |

## Analysis

List every problem with: where, how many participants hit it, severity (blocked / slowed /
annoyed). Fix blockers first. Terms that confused two or more participants go into
`translation-guide.md` with a replacement before any more content is translated.

## Ethics and data

Participation voluntary, can stop any time, no names in notes (P1…P8), recordings deleted after
analysis. Nothing in this study is shown publicly as a testimonial or a usage count
(CLAUDE.md: user-facing counts are content counts, never usage counts).
