# Proposal C — Production-first: 96 units at DaF quality, built on rails

**Date:** 2026-09-27 · **Status:** blueprint proposal, one of several angles, to be reconciled into
`docs/course-v2/BLUEPRINT.md` · **Angle:** production-first. About 96 units must be written by AI agents at
DaF-teacher quality in days, not months. This proposal designs the anatomy, the content schema and the
machine-checkable quality gates so that correct, level-appropriate German is the **default output** of the
pipeline, not the result of twenty review rounds. It reuses the infrastructure that stays (checker, AI grading,
TTS, purchases).

## 0. The proposal on one page

1. **The problem is convergence, not volume.** The rebuilt A1.1 needed **23 adversarial DaF reviews** to go from
   5 BLOCKER / 45 MAJOR to 0 / 0 for 12 Lektionen (CLAUDE.md; `docs/course-factory/a11-rebuild/REVIEW-daf-*.md`).
   Most MAJOR findings were **classes**, not typos. Review #9 alone lists a seeded practice draw that never
   re-drew, a task-shape key that masked only words of six letters or more, checkpoint *Lesen* statements that
   were verbatim substrings of their own text, and a polite-form case flag that read the answer's shape instead
   of the task. Each one was multiplied across every Lektion. Those classes now live as about 30 validator rules
   (`scripts/validate-curriculum.mjs`, RULE 1–25 plus lettered variants) and 19 exclusion reasons plus repair
   predicates (`quality.js`). Written the same way, 96 units would need 96 × "many" review rounds. **The production-first bet is to start all eight levels
   with the rules A1.1 had after round 23, not after round 1, and to turn every new finding class into a rule
   before the next unit is written** (CLAUDE.md: "close a finding class with a rule + a test, never with a list
   of ids").
2. **Rails before content (design).** Six registries are written and frozen first: can-dos, the grammar spine,
   the lexicon, exam lanes, rubric profiles and level profiles. A unit spec may only **select** registry IDs, and
   the unit's content is then written against the spec and checked against it. An author agent cannot invent a
   can-do, a grammar point, a new word or an exam format.
3. **Fixed slots, typed content (design).** Every unit has the same shape: Start · three *Situation*-Lernschritte
   · one *Prüfungstraining* · *Aufgabe* · *Abschluss*. Every slot has a template with counts and constraints.
   About a quarter of the practice items are **generated by code** from the lexicon and the grammar spine, so
   their correctness is inherited rather than written.
4. **A gate stack of 14 checks (G0–G13), all runnable in seconds.** Schema, references, allocation, lexis
   coverage, grammar ceiling, text metrics, spelling and grammar (Hunspell + LanguageTool), item well-formedness,
   a **blind solver** (a second model answers every item from what the learner sees and is graded by the real
   `check.js`), consistency, exam fidelity, legal copy lint, audio readiness and grader calibration. Two AI DaF
   reviewers, a *Prüferin* persona and a *Kursleiterin* persona, see a unit only after all of these pass. They
   score a 6 × 5 rubric (§9.5).
5. **Pilot first, then scale.** One pilot unit per level (8 units) runs the full loop to 0 BLOCKER / 0 MAJOR. Its
   finding classes become rules, and the 8 pilots become the exemplars every author prompt carries. Then 88 units
   are written in waves of 12.
6. **Numbers (design; re-plan after the pilot):**
   - **Structure:** 8 × 12 = 96 units, 32 Plateaus, 8 end tests, 7 half-mocks, 24 full mock forms.
   - **Items:** ≈ 10,000 items, of which ≈ 7,500 are written by agents and ≈ 2,500 are generated.
   - **Texts:** ≈ 480 unit texts and ≈ 290 assessment texts.
   - **Words:** 3,700–4,300 lexicon entries, of which ≈ 1,700–2,100 are new `words` rows (memo 07).
   - **Productive tasks:** 192 exam-format *Aufgaben* in the units, plus 288 AI-scored micro-outputs.
   - **Production run:** ≈ 1,660 agent runs at a peak of ≈ 40 concurrent, over ≈ 12 working days of wall
     clock, including 2 days of rails.
7. **What stays and what is new.** The checker, mastery/requeue, the review service, read-aloud scoring,
   `evaluate-writing` (made profile-driven), the speaking stack, the Modelltest runner, the JSON→SQL pipelines, the
   Azure pipeline, and purchases + `hasLevelAccess` all stay. New are: a **compiler** that turns
   `content/course-v2/**` into the shapes those pieces already consume, `scripts/course-v2/validate.mjs`, a server-owned
   speaking-task bank, per-exam scorers, and purchase-aware entitlement in the AI functions. That last item is a
   build prerequisite, because today a €50–65 buyer has no server-side right to AI grading (memo 12 §4).

## 1. Product and positioning

### 1.1 Who buys

Memo 10 is the source for this section.

- **Lead segment: the spouse-visa A1 candidate, reached first through India's English-language market.**
  - "goethe a1 exam" gets 27,100 searches a month in India, peaking at 40,500 in Jul 2026 (db snapshot).
  - The Goethe-Institut held 35,720 spouse Start Deutsch 1 exams in 2024 and 62 % passed, so there were
    ≈ 13,400 failed attempts (Drs. 21/175, https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/001/2100175.pdf).
  - The free A1.1 sits directly in front of the paid A1.2, which completes the exam.
- **Revenue core: B1 "zum Bleiben".**
  - Germany had 332,500 naturalisations in 2025
    (https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/06/PD26_186_125.html).
  - About 319,000 people took the DTZ, and 55.0 % reached B1 (Drs. 21/5716,
    https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/057/2105716.pdf).
  - German search demand runs telc ≫ DTZ > Goethe (memo 02 §5).
- **B2 serves work and care as a channel, not a lead.**
  - 32,000 nurses were recognised in 2025, and nurses need general B2 (memo 10 §2.3).
  - Doctors' Fachsprache belongs to MedMeister.

**What production-first adds for these buyers.** Explanations are written in plain English at no more than B1 and
stored **apart** from the German content (memo 10 impl. 6). In the schema (§8) every explanation is a map
`{ en, tr?, ar?, … }`. A Turkish or Arabic layer is then a translation job over one field type, with no
re-authoring of German. Turkey is the largest spouse origin, and its pass rates are below 40 % (memo 10 §2.1).

### 1.3 Why this beats the field

Memo 08 §5 ranks ten gaps. Three of them are hard to close without an industrial pipeline, and this proposal is
built around them.

- **Gap 3, real depth at B1.1–B2.2.**
  - Babbel has about 15 B1 lessons, Nicos Weg and the general vhs courses stop at B1, and TestGerman's B1–B2 is
    "coming soon" (memo 08 §4).
  - In this pipeline a B-level unit costs the same agent runs as an A-level unit, so the depth is the same.
- **Gap 1, every productive task scored inside a real course.**
  - Official material shows the rubric but never applies it. Course products do not grade, and graders barely
    teach (memo 08 §4).
  - Here every unit ends in two exam-format tasks scored on that exam's own rubric profile (§3.5).
- **Gap 5, teach → test → repair.** Every AI error tag links to a **static** rule card and to a repair drill
  (§3.5). Busuu paywalls this, and Duolingo's grammar help is its most common complaint (memo 08 §2).

The rest follows from the same data:

- Teil-by-Teil speaking (gap 2): a speaking bank with exam-interaction modes (§3.6).
- One course for the exam you booked (gap 4): lanes (§2.6).
- An honest practice score (gap 6): per-lane scorers on the real pass rule (§4).
- A dated plan (gap 7).
- A published **Inhaltsverzeichnis** per half-level, generated from `course.json` (memo 14 G7: every book
  publishes one and no app does).

### 1.4 The legal edge, turned into gates

Memo 13 sets the constraints. Every one of them is a check in G11 (§9.1), not a reviewer's memory.

- **Self-study material plus automated practice software.**
  - No page, checkout or e-mail offers a right to ask content questions, a human correction or progress
    monitoring by "us".
  - Support is limited to "Zugang, Technik, Rechnung" (memo 13 impl. 1–2).
- **AI grading is formative and labelled on every surface:** „Automatische KI-Auswertung — keine Korrektur durch
  eine Lehrkraft, kein Prüfungsergebnis. Richtwert." Progression is **never gated on an AI score**; unit gates are
  deterministic, and the learner can override them (memo 13 impl. 3–4; AI Act Annex III 3(b)).
- **No runtime AI that answers questions about course material in paid courses** (memo 13 impl. 5).
  - **Production-first makes this cheap (design):** every explanation, rule card and feedback line for a
    discrete item is written at authoring time and stored as static text. Runtime AI appears only in role-play
    speaking and in rubric scoring of the learner's own production.
  - The current `explain-answer` function stays off in paid v2 courses until counsel clears it.
- **Other rules:**
  - The completion document is a *Teilnahmebescheinigung* only (memo 13 impl. 7).
  - Exam names appear only in purpose phrases (impl. 8).
  - No outcome claims (impl. 9).
  - TTS audio is labelled „Computerstimme" (impl. 11).
  - An AI-interaction notice appears at first contact, per Art. 50(1) AI Act, which applies since 2 Aug 2026
    (memo 13 §4).

## 2. Course architecture

### 2.1 The frame (same for all eight)

- **12 units per half-level, with a Plateau after units 3, 6, 9 and 12 (design).**
  - Menschen has 12 Lektionen per Teilband with a Modul-Plus every 3; Lingoda has 12 chapters per sub-level;
    Netzwerk neu has a Plateau every 3 Kapitel (memo 14 §B, §G1).
  - One unit is one situational can-do cluster, about one week at standard pace (memo 09 impl. 1).
- **The .1 courses end with an exam-format half-mock** on their own content after Plateau 4.
- **The .2 courses follow an exam arc (memo 09 impl. 11; memo 08 impl. 3):**
  - a diagnostic mini-mock in week 1 ("Standortbestimmung", about 30 min);
  - three full timed mocks, placed by the plan after units 10, 11 and 12 or backwards from the exam date;
  - a final "Prüfungswoche" with review only and no new content.
- **Unit anatomy is identical across levels.** Only the **contents** of each slot scale, and the level profile
  (§8.2) decides how.

### 2.4 Grammar allocation approach

**One registry, `grammar-spine.json` (design).** Each point carries:

- `id`;
- a German label for the rule card;
- `intro.receptive` and `intro.productive`, each a unit id;
- `detectors[]`, which are ids of construction detectors (§9.2);
- `contrast`, its confusable partner (for example `g.denn` ↔ `g.weil`, `g.wenn` ↔ `g.als`);
- `errorTags[]`, taken from memo 06 impl. 15;
- `lehrwerk[]` and `consensus` (strong / majority / split).

The tables above give 12–16 new points per half-level, which is the consensus size of 11–15 (memo 06 impl. 1),
plus the labelled *Wiederholungen*.

**The hinge decisions from memo 06, recorded once in the registry:**

1. **Perfekt:** chunks at the end of A1.1 (U11), systematic in A1.2 (U6, U11). This covers both the Hueber and
   the Klett placement (memo 06 impl. 2).
2. **Dativ:** fixed-case prepositional phrases first (A1.2 U5), then dative verbs and pronouns (U8, U9)
   (impl. 4).
3. **Wechselpräpositionen:** taught through verb pairs (A2.1 U2). Feedback names the verb, not only
   Wo?/Wohin? (impl. 5).
4. **weil / dass / wenn:** A2.1 (impl. 6).
5. **Passiv:** receptive in A2.2 U6, productive in B1.2 U7–8 (impl. 7).
6. **Relativsatz:** Nom/Akk A2.2 U8 → Dativ/with preposition B1.1 U3 → wo/was B1.2 U6 → wer/was B2.1 U6
   (impl. 8).
7. **Futur I:** B1.1 U9 (impl. 9).
8. **Präteritum:** war/hatte A1.2 U1 → modal verbs A2.1 U7 → receptive A2.2 U7 → narrative B1.1 U1 (impl. 10).
9. **Konjunktiv II ladder:** chunks A1.2 → *sollte/könnte* A2.1 U5 → *wäre/hätte/würde* A2.2 U1 → unreal
   conditions B1.1 U10 → past receptive B1.2 U11 → past productive B2.1 U7 (impl. 11).
10. **Two-part connectors:** B1.2 U1, reviewed in B2.1 U2. **Konjunktiv I:** B2.2, where both books put it.

**Rule cards are written once per spine point per level of depth (design), not once per unit.**

- A card has at most 80 words (at A1, 60) in German at the learner's level, plus an English twin.
- It opens with the model sentence (Schritte's "one structure per step", memo 14 steal 2).
- It may make no unconditioned claims (the `unconditionedRule` class in `quality.js`).
- The same card is re-shown at every *Wiederholung*, so ≈ 110 cards serve 96 units.
- The grammar DB's 672 rules and 933 examples (memo 12 §1) are a quarry for card drafts after the quality filter.
  Their grammar-first sequencing is not inherited.

## 3. Unit anatomy and lesson anatomy

### 3.1 The principle: fixed slots, typed content (design)

A unit is a fixed sequence of **slots**. Every slot has:

- a template (what the author fills in);
- counts (how many items of which kind);
- constraints (length bands, the known-word set, the grammar ceiling);
- a gate list (§9).

The anatomy unites the Schritte and Menschen skeleton for A levels with the Aspekte and Sicher! skeleton for
B levels (memo 14 G4–G5), cut to the evidence-based 20-minute segment (memo 09 impl. 2) and the "page the
practice, scroll the input" rule (memo 11 §2).

| Slot | Minutes A / B | What the learner does | Authored items | Generated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Start** | 2 / 3 | Lernziele box (3–5 can-dos from the registry); *Einstieg*: at A a 30–60 s *Folge* teaser of the serial cast, at B an ambiguous photo plus a 60 s free-speaking prompt (ungraded, fluency only; Sicher's Einstiegsseite, memo 14 steal 18); „Ich kann das schon" test-out | 0 | test-out reuses the can-do proofs |
| **LS1–LS3 · Situation** (×3) | 20 / 25 each | 1 warm-up → 2 input → 3 form → 4 practice → 5 micro-output → 6 exit (table below) | 27 items + 1 micro-task per LS | ≈ 6 of the 18 practice items |
| **Aussprache** (inside LS1 and LS3) | 2 each | 4 perception items with ≥ 4 voices (HVPT, memo 09 §9), then read-aloud of the model sentence, scored for intelligibility | 1 read-aloud line | 4 perception items from lexicon minimal pairs |
| **LS4 · Prüfungstraining** | 20 / 30 | 1–2 exam Teile in the home lane at official text sizes; a strategy card (≤ 60 words); exam-mode play counts | 8 (A) / 10 (B) | 0 |
| **Aufgabe** | 25 / 35 | 1 exam-format *Schreiben* + 1 exam-format *Sprechen*, each run as plan → attempt → automatic feedback → one revision | 2 tasks (+ checklist, model text, moves) | 0 |
| **Abschluss** | 5 | „Das kann ich": 1 proof item per can-do; Lernwortschatz into SRS; next review date | 3–5 | review cards (all) |
| *Fokus* (optional) | 10–15 | DaZ or DaF side task (Amt, Kita, Hotel …) | 1 task | 0 |

**Per unit (design):** ≈ 104 items, of which ≈ 26 are generated, plus 2 exam-format tasks, 3 micro-tasks, 4–6
texts and 1 model text. In-lesson time is **≈ 112 min at A** and **≈ 148 min at B**, plus ≈ 30 min of daily
review a week (6 × 5 min). That gives the memo 09 unit of about one week at ≈ 2.4–3 h.

**Design hours per half-level (derived from the minutes above; to be measured before any claim):**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3 | Form | 3 / 4 | Rule card (≤ 60 / 80 words, English twin), the model sentence, 4 structured-input items where the form carries the meaning | Learner fills the rule table from the text, then the card is revealed (memo 14 steal 16) | 4 |

| 5 | Micro-output | 3–4 / 4–5 | 30 s plan → 20–40 s spoken **or** 1–3 typed sentences → automatic mini-feedback → one revision | 40–60 s spoken or 40–60 words typed | 1 task (alternates speaking / writing across LS1–LS3) |
| 6 | Exit | 1 | 3 items not yet seen, from the pool; next review date shown | same | 3 (from the pool) |

- **Recall share.** At least 70 % of the served practice items are recall formats: typed gap, sentence building,
  dictation, error correction, notes (memo 09 impl. 3). The gate counts this per LS.
- **New words per LS.** At A levels, ≈ 9–10 new words per LS (≈ 28–30 per unit); at B2, up to ≈ 20, of which
  ≥ 60 % are receptive (memo 07).

### 3.3 The Prüfungstraining (LS4) and the Aufgabe

**LS4** is written against the **lane Teil template** (§8.2):

- item count and option count;
- text length band;
- play count;
- time box.

The .1 courses may mark a block `scaffolded` (fewer distractors, slower audio in learning mode). The .2 courses
may not. Each LS4 ends with a *strategy card* of at most 60 words. An example for SD1 Hören Teil 2:
„Sie hören den Text nur einmal. Lesen Sie zuerst die Aussage. Achten Sie auf Zahlen und Orte."

**Aufgabe.** Two exam-format productions follow the rubric profile of the home lane. Both run the memo 09
impl. 14 loop:

1. Attempt.
2. Self-correction prompts only (e.g. „Prüfen Sie die Verbposition nach *denn*.").
3. Revision.
4. Second automatic evaluation.
5. Only then the reformulation of at most 3 errors, and the model text.

The AI never shows a rewritten text before the learner's own revision (memo 09 §5, Fan et al.).

### 3.5 How writing is graded

**Step 1, the deterministic pre-check.** It runs in the client, is twinned on the server, and takes under 100 ms
(memo 11 impl. 17). It is shown as a checklist **before** submission and never blocks.

| Check | Rule source | Example message |
|---|---|---|
| Word count vs the profile band | memos 01–03 | „Ca. 30 Wörter — Sie haben 17." |
| Zero-rule warning (GA2/GB1/GB2: < 50 % of words = task scores 0) | memos 01–03 | „Nach der Regel des Goethe-Zertifikats B1 wird ein Text unter 40 Wörtern mit 0 Punkten bewertet." |
| Anrede / Gruß / Betreff present, per text type | memos 01–03 | „Es fehlt ein Gruß am Ende." |
| Register drift (du and Sie forms mixed) | tB1 scale (memo 02) | „Sie schreiben erst *Sie*, dann *du*." |
| Share of sentences starting with *Ich/Wir* > 50 % (telc B1/B2) | memo 02 | „Viele Sätze beginnen mit *Ich*." |
| Leitpunkt cue coverage (author-given cue lemmas per Leitpunkt; advisory, the AI decides) | design | „Punkt 3 (Frage) — noch kein Fragesatz." |
| Anrede/Gruß is **never** a Leitpunkt | A1.1 DaF review #1, MAJOR | spec-gate error, not learner-facing |

**Step 2, the rubric-profile call (design; generalises `evaluate-writing`).**

- **The request.** `evaluate-writing` today scores every task on one fixed 4 × 0–5 scale and gives
  simple-German feedback only for `goethe_a1` (memo 12 §3). v2 adds a `rubricProfile` field to each bank task.
  The function loads the profile's criteria, scales, zero-rules and feedback language.
- **The prompt layout.** The system block holds the profile text plus 3 anchor texts. It is identical for every
  call of that profile, so it is cached (prefix caching, 0.1 × input price;
  https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing).
- **The output (JSON):**
  - points per criterion, on the exam's own scale;
  - `leitpunkt_check[]`;
  - at most 3 errors `{span, tag, ruleCardId, hint}`, tagged with memo 06 impl. 15's categories (V2/INV,
    verb-final, Satzklammer, case in NP vs PP, gender, adjective ending, Perfekt aux/participle, connector
    position, n-Deklination);
  - `strengths[2]` and one `nextStep`.
- **Weighting by level.** An inversion error at B1 is flagged, not scored as a level failure (memo 06 impl. 15,
  MERLIN).
- **Feedback language by level profile:**
  - A1–A2: short simple German (≤ 60 words) plus the English twin;
  - B1+: German.

**Profiles (from memos 01–03; the scales are the exams' published ones):**

| Profile | Criteria and scale | Hard rules in the pre-check |
|---|---|---|
| `sd1-s2` (also `ta2-s2`, 3 of 4 points) | Leitpunkt 3 / 1.5 / 0 × 3 + Textsorte 1 / 0.5 / 0 = 10; spelling only if it hurts understanding | ≈ 30 words (tA2 ≈ 40) |
| `oesd-za1-s2` | deductions: 20–24 words −1, 15–19 −2, < 15 = 0; content −1/−2/0 | word bands |
| `ga2-s1`, `ga2-s2` | Aufgabenerfüllung + Sprache, each 5 / 3.5 / 2 / 0.5 / 0 | E (< 50 % words) zeroes the task |

**Step 3, labels and limits (memo 13).**

- Every result card carries the fixed line and shows the exam's own points ("7,5 von 10 Punkten (Richtwert)"),
  never a bare percentage (memo 01 impl. 3).
- Unlimited retries are allowed, within a fair-use cap.
- Results are private to the learner and never gate progress.

**Cost (derived; model per profile is a config choice to be fixed by the calibration suite, §9.4).** List prices
per MTok (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing, read 2026-09-27): Sonnet 4.6 $3 in / $15 out,
Sonnet 5 $2 / $10, Haiku 4.5 $1 / $5, cache hits 0.1 × input.

- **A full-rubric call** (~2,000 tokens in, of which ~1,400 are a cached profile prefix, and ~800 out) costs
  ≈ $0.014 on Sonnet 4.6 and ≈ $0.009 on Sonnet 5, at steady-state cache hits.
- **A micro call** (~900 in, ~200 out on Haiku 4.5) costs ≈ $0.002.
- **A fully engaged learner per half-level** makes ≈ 37 full writing calls and ≈ 72 micro calls. That is
  ≈ $0.66 on Sonnet 4.6.
- **Speaking** adds ≈ 33 sessions. Their STT + turn + evaluation cost is **unmeasured**; memo 12 lists STT at
  ≈ $0.003/min (snippet).
- **Order of magnitude:** a few dollars per fully engaged learner per €40–65 course. This is **to be measured in
  the pilot** before "unlimited" appears in any copy.

### 3.6 How speaking is graded

**One speaking bank, server-owned (design).**

- **Why.** Today a course task travels as client text, capped at 300 characters with ≤ 8 hint words

  "must never grade a client-supplied prompt" (`src/data/writingTasks.js` header).
- **What v2 adds.** A twin pair `src/data/speakingTasks.js` ↔ `netlify/functions/_shared/speakingTasks.mjs`
  (drift-guarded by `check-duplicates.mjs`), keyed `<course>-uNN-sp`. Each entry holds:
  - `mode`;

  - `cards[]` (for card modes);
  - `moves[]`;
  - `hintWords` (≤ 8);
  - `prepMinutes`;

- **The mission machinery stays** (evaluation with pass criteria, opening line) (memo 12 §2.3).

### 3.7 Worked example: A1.2 Unit 4 „Ich bin krank" (learner view)

**Cast (A1 bible, design).**

- **Priya Nair**, 28, from Kochi, India. She speaks Malayalam and English, recently joined her husband **Arjun**
  (software developer) in Leipzig, and works part-time in the Café Lindner.
- **Frau Lindner**, her boss (Sie).
- **Mehmet Aydın**, a classmate from İzmir (du among learners).
- **Frau Schulz**, the course teacher (Sie).
- **Dr. Wagner**, the family doctor.

**Start.** Lernziele: „Am Ende der Lektion können Sie …

- sich am Telefon krankmelden,
- sagen, was Ihnen wehtut,
- sich in einer kurzen Nachricht entschuldigen und einen Grund nennen."

*Einstieg*: 40 s of *Folge 16*. Priya wakes up, coughs, and looks at the clock.

**LS1 „Auf dem Anrufbeantworter" (structure: *denn*).**

- **Input** (audio, 2 voices, labelled „Computerstimme"):
  - Ansage: „Guten Tag, hier ist das Café Lindner. Leider sind wir gerade nicht da. Bitte sprechen Sie nach dem
    Signalton."
  - Priya: „Hallo Frau Lindner, hier ist Priya Nair. Es tut mir leid, ich kann heute nicht arbeiten, denn ich
    bin krank. Ich habe Fieber und Halsschmerzen. Ich gehe heute Nachmittag zum Arzt. Ich rufe Sie morgen
    wieder an. Auf Wiederhören!"
- **Gist:** „Warum ruft Priya an?" (a) Sie ist krank. (b) Sie hat keine Zeit. (c) Sie kommt später.
- **Detail:** „Wohin geht Priya heute Nachmittag?" → „zum Arzt".
- **Rule card** (≤ 60 words): „Mit **denn** nennen Sie einen Grund. Nach **denn** steht das Verb auf Position 2 —
  wie im normalen Satz: *Ich kann nicht arbeiten, denn ich **bin** krank.*"
  English twin: "Use *denn* to give a reason. The word order after *denn* does not change."
- **Structured input** (the form decides the meaning): „Mehmet: Ich komme heute nicht, denn mein Sohn ist
  krank. — Wer ist krank?" (a) Mehmet (b) Mehmets Sohn.
- **Practice** (3 of the 18):
  - `fill_blank` „Priya kann nicht arbeiten, ___ sie hat Fieber." → `denn`. Only *denn* fits the verb-second
    clause; the blind solver will confirm there is no second key.
  - `sentence_building` „Ich komme heute nicht, … [ich / krank / denn / bin]" → accepted:
    `denn ich bin krank`, `denn krank bin ich`. The second order is grammatical, so it must be accepted
    (`missingFrontedOrder`).
  - `error_correction` „Ich komme nicht, denn ich krank bin." → „Ich komme nicht, denn ich bin krank."
    (tag *Verbstellung*).
- **Micro-output** (spoken, 20–30 s): „Sie sind krank. Sprechen Sie auf den Anrufbeantworter von Ihrer Chefin:
  Wer sind Sie? Warum rufen Sie an? Wann kommen Sie wieder?" Profile `course-micro`: are the three points there,
  is *denn* used, is it intelligible.
- **Aussprache:** perception of *Hals – Haus* and of the *ach*/*ich* sounds in *Bauch – Bäuche*, across
  4 voices, then read-aloud of the model sentence.

**LS2 „Was tut Ihnen weh?"**

- Structure: *tut weh / tun weh*, with *mein/meine* reviewed from A1.1.
- Model sentence: „Mein Kopf tut weh. Meine Füße tun weh."
- Generated items come from the lexicon compounds *Kopf → Kopfschmerzen*, *Hals → Halsschmerzen*,
  *Bauch → Bauchschmerzen*, using the template „Mein Kopf tut weh. = Ich habe ___."

**LS3 „Eine Nachricht an Frau Schulz".** The learner reads Mehmet's model message (32 words, the
Mitteilung text type). It is checked by the same pre-check the learner's own text will get (RULE 17 carried
forward):

> Liebe Frau Schulz,
> ich kann heute nicht zum Kurs kommen, denn mein Sohn ist krank. Ich bleibe zu Hause. Am Mittwoch komme ich
> wieder. Was sind die Hausaufgaben?
> Viele Grüße
> Mehmet Aydın

**LS4 Prüfungstraining.** SD1 Hören Teil 3 (answering machine, 2×, 5 items) plus SD1 Lesen Teil 3 (signs,
3 items). Sample H3 message:

> „Hallo, hier ist die Praxis Dr. Wagner. Ihr Termin am Montag um neun Uhr geht leider nicht. Können Sie am
> Dienstag um halb elf kommen? Bitte rufen Sie uns an."

„Wann ist der neue Termin?" (a) Montag, 9 Uhr (b) Dienstag, 10.30 Uhr (c) Dienstag, 11.30 Uhr.

- **Key:** b.
- **G9 time sanity** checks that *halb elf* = 10:30. This is the classic A1 trap, and review #9 found the old
  course never drilled *halb*.

**Aufgabe.**

- **Schreiben** (profile `sd1-s2`): „Sie sind krank und können am Freitag nicht arbeiten. Schreiben Sie Ihrer
  Chefin, Frau Lindner, eine E-Mail:
  – Warum schreiben Sie?
  – Wann kommen Sie wieder?
  – Fragen Sie: Kann eine Kollegin am Freitag für Sie arbeiten?"
  - ≈ 30 words.
  - Checklist: Anrede, three points, Gruß, Sie-Form. Polite *Sie/Ihnen* are `caseSensitive`, because here the
    capital letter is the task (CLAUDE.md, checker rule).
- **Sprechen** (profile `sd1-sp3`, mode `cards-request`): four picture cards (Taschentuch, Tee, Tablette,
  Fenster).
  - „Können Sie mir bitte einen Tee geben?" → the AI answers „Ja, gern. Hier, bitte."
  - The AI then asks „Können Sie bitte das Fenster aufmachen?" → the learner reacts.
  - Scoring: 2 points per request, 1 per reaction.

**Abschluss.** „Das kann ich":

- „Ich kann mich am Telefon krankmelden." → proof: listen to three messages and choose the Krankmeldung.
- „Ich kann sagen, was mir wehtut." → proof: `fill_blank` „Ihr Bauch tut weh. Sie sagen: Ich habe ___."
- „Ich kann mich schriftlich entschuldigen und einen Grund nennen." → proof: the *Aufgabe* submission itself.
  Completing it is recorded; the score is not.

**Fokus-DaZ** (optional): „Die Krankmeldung bei der Arbeit". It carries a sourced fact with a date:

> „Dauert die Arbeitsunfähigkeit länger als drei Kalendertage, hat der Arbeitnehmer eine ärztliche
> Bescheinigung … vorzulegen"

(§ 5 Abs. 1 EntgFG, https://dejure.org/gesetze/EntgFG/5.html, `factsCheckedOn: 2026-09-27`). Like the Leitfäden
(CLAUDE.md), every factual claim in course data carries `sources` and `factsCheckedOn`, or it is not allowed.

### 3.8 Compressed example: B1.2 Unit 1 „Meine Meinung im Forum"

- **Start.** Photo prompt: a parcel mountain in a stairwell. „Was passiert hier? Was denken Sie?" (60 s, fluency

- **LS1 · Text type "Forumsbeitrag".** Readers' posts, each ≈ 60 words, about „Online kaufen oder im Laden?".

  > „Ich kaufe Kleidung fast nur online, denn es spart Zeit. Trotzdem finde ich, dass der Laden um die Ecke

  > Meiner Meinung nach brauchen wir beides."
- **LS2 · Radio vox-pop "Wer sagt was?"** (GB1 H4 / tB1 HV1 style): *sowohl … als auch*.
- **LS3 · Leserbrief with a counter-argument:** *darum / deswegen*.
- **LS4:** GB1 Lesen Teil 4 (7 comments, Ja/Nein) plus a tB1 Sprachbausteine cloze of 10 gaps as the Lane-Extra.
- **Aufgabe:**
  - GB1 Sch2 (profile `gb1-sch2`, ≈ 80 words, zero rule below 40);
  - `plan-together` for a Tauschbörse im Haus, with the moves checklist.
- **Spiral tag:** *Meinung*, band step B1 → B1+ („zusammenfassen und Stellung nehmen", memo 05).

| .2 **full mocks** | 3 parallel forms per lane, at full timing, with play counts and an answer-transfer step on one paper-style form (telc A1/A2 are paper only, memo 01) | authored against the lane form blueprint (§8.3) | per-lane scorer | none |
| **Abschlusstest** | 60 min, all four skills, scaled down; completion unlocks the Teilnahmebescheinigung, which carries no score (memo 13 impl. 7) | compiled + authored listening | per-course scorer | completion only |
| **Einstufung** | ≤ 10-min adaptive test with partial credit plus a can-do self-check; it recommends a course (memo 11 impl. 8) | **the Plateau item banks, which have no separate authoring**: 32 banks tagged by half-level | adaptive stepping between Plateau banks | none |

**Per-lane scorers apply the real pass rule, never a generic 60 % (memos 01–03).**

| Lane | Rule implemented |
|---|---|
| Goethe A1 (SD1) | raw × 1.66 → 100; pass ≥ 60 with every part taken; no part floor (below 35 written the oral cannot rescue) |
| telc A1 / A2 | 60 points; 36 = ausreichend |
| ÖSD ZA1 | written ≥ 38/75, with Lesen ≥ 6, Hören ≥ 6 and Schreiben ≥ 4; oral ≥ 12/25; modules separate |
| Goethe A2 | ≥ 60/100 **and** ≥ 45/75 written **and** ≥ 15/25 oral |
| Goethe/ÖSD B1, Goethe B2 | each module ≥ 60/100 on its own; Lesen/Hören raw × 3.33, so 18/30 = 60; no compensation between modules |
| telc B1 / B2 | ≥ 135/225 written **and** ≥ 45/75 oral; uneven points per item (B1: 5 / 2.5 / 1.5) |
| DTZ | H+L ≥ 33/45, Schreiben ≥ 15/20, Sprechen ≥ 75/100; B1 needs Sprechen B1 plus one of the other two; Sprechen below A2 = no certificate |

**The readiness display, „Übungsstand je Teil" (design).**

- It shows the best of the last two attempts per Teil on the exam's own scale, with a hairline at the pass rule.
- It is worded „Nach der Bestehensregel von Goethe (60 von 100 Punkten) läge dieses Übungsergebnis darüber —
  Ergebnis in unserer Übungsprüfung, kein Prüfungsergebnis." (memo 01 impl. 2; memo 13 impl. 9).
- **It never shows a probability.** Retest effects inflate mock scores: d = .26, larger with identical forms
  (memo 09 §1).
- Mocks are therefore **parallel forms**, and the equivalence check in G10 (§9.1) compares the forms' text length
  (± 10 %), lexical coverage and item-type mix.

**The retake path at A1.2 and B1.2 (memo 10 impl. 4).** The learner types in the official per-part score
(SD1 / DTZ / telc) and gets a 4-week plan weighted to the weak Teile. The plan draws every block tagged with those
Teile across all units, which is **a query over the data model, not new content**.

## 5. Review and retention

**Cards are generated, never authored (design).** The compiler emits card keys the existing `reviewService`
already stores (`review_cards.card_key`):

- `word:<lemmaId>`: productive words get two-way cards, receptive words recognition-only cards (memo 07 impl. 10);
- `pattern:<unitId>:<ls>`: the model sentence of every Situation-LS;
- `sentence:<itemId>`: a missed typed item;
- `repair:<errorTag>:<ruleCardId>`: created from a graded production's error list. This is the teach → test →
  repair loop, and it is included in the price (memo 08 impl. 6).

**The ladder** (memo 09 impl. 7–9):

- The existing `src/lib/review/ladder.js` runs 1 / 4 / 7 / 14 / 60 / 180 days, and a lapse returns to the start.
- **v2 makes the ladder a course parameter (design):** +1 / +3 / +7 / +14 / +30.
- Each interval is capped at max(1 day, 15 % of the days left to the exam), per Cepeda's ridge.
- Targets: ≥ 5 spaced retrievals for productive items and ≥ 3 for receptive items before the exam date.
- New grammar gets 1–3-day lags first.
- **Interleaving uses the spine's `contrast` field:** from a point's second LS onwards, warm-ups mix it with its
  partner (*denn/weil*, *wenn/als*, Akkusativ/Dativ, Perfekt *haben/sein*). The UI warns that scores dip while
  contrasts are mixed (memo 09 §3).
- **Words are blocked in the unit and interleaved only in review.** Interleaving words gave g = −0.39 in Brunmair
  & Richter (memo 09 §3).
- An A/B test of the fixed ladder against an adaptive scheduler (HLR/FSRS) is instrumented, not assumed
  (memo 09 impl. 23).

**Re-entry.** After three or more days away the learner gets a 5-minute review, not the backlog (memo 09
impl. 22). The daily review runs 5–6 minutes, and its count only shrinks within a session (memo 11 S5).

## 6. Engagement and the player UX

Memo 11 covers the evidence. Production-first adds one rule: **the player contains no level-specific code.**
Every screen reads unit data, and every per-level constant (voices, manifests, known-word sets, meta) is emitted
by the compiler. Today this is where the A1.1 couplings live: `speech.js` manifests, `IntroStage`,
`WortfeldStage`, the `lexis.js` tables and the pool loaders (memo 12 §3).

- **One practice item per screen, scrolling input** (memo 11 impl. 1).
- **One primary action**, pinned in the thumb zone, ≥ 48 px high.
- **Floor and budget.** Design floor 360 × 640, reflow at 320 px. The player route stays within ≈ 0.62 MiB of JS,
  and unit data is fetched per unit as JSON (≈ 20–40 KB, design), not bundled (memo 11 impl. 3).
- **Progress and syllabus.** The progress bar counts first presentations only; a re-queued miss shows as
  „+1 Wiederholung". Goals nest: item → LS → unit ring → course (units and can-dos) → „Übungsstand je Teil". There
  is no XP, no hearts, no leagues and no loot (memo 11 impl. 4–5, 20).
- **The syllabus is always visible**, with can-do titles. Unbought half-levels appear as locked chapters on the
  same map, and the course's Inhaltsverzeichnis page comes from the same `course.json` (memo 11 impl. 6).
- **The serial cast is data.** There is one cast bible per CEFR band (A1, A2, B1, B2) holding persona facts,
  relationships (du/Sie) and voice assignments. Each unit's *Folge* continues the story in 2 minutes or less with
  a task on every clip (memo 14 steal 3, avoid 5). G9 enforces persona consistency (RULE 14 generalised).
  - **Voices:** existing Katja and Conrad (`generate-course-audio.mjs`), plus further German neural voices. At
    B1–B2, de-AT Ingrid/Jonas and de-CH Leni are available
    (https://json2video.com/ai-voices/azure/languages/german/) for D-A-CH exposure, e.g. in B2.2 U11.

- **Habit.**
  - The streak counts learning days, and its unit is one Lernschritt or the daily review.
  - Two free, automatic Pausentage per week; earn-back within 48 h; the number is shown, never a broken "0".
  - The weekly goal („3 von 3 Lerntagen") is the headline metric (memo 11 impl. 10–13).
  - Reminders: at most one a day, by e-mail by default, ≥ 6 rotating templates per kind, all sent through

- **Plan and report.**
  - The goal screen asks for the exam (type + date, or „noch kein Termin"), days per week and a reminder time.

  - A weekly recorded report has an opt-in share link (impl. 16).

  speaking micro-output**: every U1 LS1 micro-output is spoken. It ends in the exam-date plan. A1.1 U1 is playable

- **Instrumentation that proves the moat.** Events: `micro_output_submitted`, `aufgabe_submitted`,
  `aufgabe_revised`, `speaking_task_completed`, `mock_completed`. They are joined to `weekly_truth_metrics()`

## 8. Content data model

### 8.1 File layout (design)

The content lives in `content/course-v2/`, next to the existing `content/reading/`. It is plain JSON, which agents
write easily and a schema can check. A compiler turns it into the shapes the engine already consumes (§8.6).

```
content/course-v2/
  registries/
    cando.json            # ≈ 450 can-dos: our ich-Form wording, source tags, band, half-level, mode
    grammar-spine.json    # ≈ 110 points: intro positions, detectors, contrast partner, error tags, Lehrwerk refs
    exam-lanes.json       # lanes → modules → Teil templates → form blueprints → pass rule, each with source + "stand"
    rubric-profiles.json  # ≈ 40 writing/speaking profiles: criteria, scales, zero rules, feedback language, anchors
    level-profiles.json   # 8 profiles: sentence metrics, register, partner support, lexis targets, text bands
    text-types.json       # ≈ 30 Textsorten: required parts (Betreff/Anrede/Gruß …), length band per level
    detectors.json        # construction detectors (§9.2), extending src/data/curricula/constructions.js
  casts/  a1.json a2.json b1.json b2.json     # persona facts, relationships (du/Sie), voices
  a1.1/ … b2.2/
    course.json           # meta, unit order, Plateaus, mocks, lanes, design hours, copy facts
    lexicon.json          # the level's lemma entries (§8.4)
    rule-cards.json       # cards for spine points entering at this level
    units/u01.json … u12.json
    plateaus/p1.json … p4.json
    mocks/<lane>-<n>.json   # .1: half-mock; .2: diagnostic + 3 forms per lane
    endtest.json
  anchors/<profile>/*.json  # grader calibration texts (§9.4)
private/  (outside the public repo)   # list_ref reference tables built from the Goethe lists; shingle hashes of official model sets
```

**IDs are stable and hierarchical:** `a1.2-u04-ls1-p07` means level, unit, slot, kind and sequence. An edit that
keeps the meaning keeps the ID, so `review_cards` keys and learner history survive re-authoring. An item whose
meaning changes gets a new ID. Every file carries `version` and `reviewedIn`, the review round that last passed it.

### 8.6 The compiler: content in, existing engine shapes out (design)

`node scripts/course-v2/compile.mjs <level>` is deterministic and idempotent, and its outputs are committed:

## 9. Quality gates

### 9.1 The gate stack

`node scripts/course-v2/validate.mjs <level> [--unit N]` runs G0–G12. G13 runs separately because it calls the

- **Hard** gates allow 0 findings.
- **Ratchet** gates follow the rule CLAUDE.md sets for A1.1: they only go down and must equal the measurement.
- **Advisory** gates route findings to the reviewers.

### 9.2 Construction detectors: what makes "level-appropriate" checkable

Each spine point names its detectors, and each detector declares its **precision class**. Exact detectors can
block; heuristic ones only route to review, because morphology tagging is ≈ 91 % accurate (spaCy card, above).

| Construction | Detector | Precision |
|---|---|---|
| unambiguous subordinators (*weil, dass, wenn, ob, obwohl, nachdem, bevor, falls, sodass, indem*) | token list + clause boundary | exact |
| ambiguous subordinators (*als* also comparative; *während, seit, bis* also prepositions; *damit* also an adverb) | token + verb-final clause check | heuristic |
| two-part connectors (*entweder … oder, weder … noch, nicht nur … sondern auch, sowohl … als auch, zwar … aber, je … desto*) | paired tokens in one sentence | exact |
| Konjunktiv II forms (*wäre, hätte, würde, könnte, müsste, dürfte* …) | closed list; *möchte* whitelisted as a chunk from A1.1 | exact |
| Präteritum of full verbs | forms from lexicon `verb_forms.praet`, minus *war/hatte* and modal verbs | exact (lexicon-driven) |
| Perfekt | auxiliary form + a participle from lexicon `verb_forms.perfekt` in the same clause | exact (lexicon-driven) |
| Passiv (werden + Partizip II; *worden*) | auxiliary + participle list | exact for *worden*; heuristic otherwise (*werden* + participle can be an adjective) |
| Futur I | *werden* + infinitive in clause-final position | heuristic |
| zu-Infinitiv | *zu* + infinitive / *-zu-* infix from separable lemmas | exact (lexicon-driven) |
| Genitive (*des … -(e)s*; *wegen/trotz/während* + Gen.) | pattern + lexicon | heuristic |
| relative clause | comma + d-pronoun + verb-final | heuristic (+ spaCy parse, advisory) |
| imperative du/ihr | clause-initial verb form without subject | heuristic |
| attributive adjective endings | determiner + adjective + noun from lexicon | heuristic |
| comparison (*-er als*, *am -sten*) | pattern | exact |
| Konjunktiv I (*sei, habe, werde* 3sg in reported speech) | pattern | advisory |
| extended participial attribute | determiner … participle + ending + noun | advisory |

The detectors generalise the existing `constructions.js`, which already backs RULE 15b and the rule-card guard
(`src/data/curricula/constructions.js` header). **Its rule carries over unchanged: the introducing unit is read
from the spine, never typed into the detector**, so moving a unit moves the guard with it.

### 9.3 The blind solver protocol (G8)

1. **The solver sees exactly what the learner sees.** That is the prompt, the options, and the text or the
   transcript of the audio. It never sees the explanation, the key or `accepted`.
2. **Two runs, graded by the real checker.** Two independent runs (different model or different prompt framing)
   each return an answer, graded by `checkAnswer(answer, accepted, checkOptionsFor(item))`, the code the learner
   meets.
3. **Pass.** Both answers are CORRECT or TYPO.
4. **Triage.** Anything else goes to a triage agent, which classifies it as one of:
   - **key wrong**: a BLOCKER;
   - **ambiguous**: rewrite the item;
   - **valid alternative**: add it to `accepted` with an `acceptedWhy`, which a reviewer must confirm;
   - **solver error**: a third run decides.
5. **Second-key probe.** For MC and sentence building, a solver is asked: „Ist eine andere Antwort ebenfalls
   richtig?"
6. **Text-blind probe.** For each R/F or abc set, a solver gets the statements without the text. Accuracy above
   60 % over the set means the set leaks. This is the class of review #9's substring finding.

**Why this gate matters most.** It replaces the A1.1 reviewers' costliest manual work, whose outcome classes are
"answer does not follow from the German prompt", unaccepted valid orders and polite-form case. And it uses the
same checker as production, so "correct" means the same thing in QA and in the app.

### 9.4 Grader calibration suite (G13)

- **Anchors.** Each rubric profile gets ≥ 12 anchor texts (writing) or transcripts (speaking) spread across its
  bands. We write them, modelled on the published band descriptors and on typical learner errors.
- **Error-heavy anchors are mandatory.** Goethe's own B2 Modellsatz labels error-heavy learner texts as B2 (memo 03
  §1). A grader tuned to surface errors under-scores real learners, so the anchors must include error-heavy texts
  that deserve passing bands. Official sample texts are copyrighted; if they are used for calibration at all,
  they stay in `private/`.
- **Expected scores.** Two independent *Prüferin*-persona agents set them and reconcile the differences. An
  optional human DaF rater can check a sample; that is internal quality work, not a learner entitlement (memo 13
  impl. 2).
- **Thresholds (design).** Each anchor runs 3 times.
  - Exact band agreement ≥ 70 %.
  - Within one band ≥ 95 %.
  - Run-to-run spread ≤ 1 band.
  - Zero-rule triggers 100 %; these come from the deterministic pre-check.
- **When the suite re-runs.** On any change of prompt, model or profile. The model id is pinned per profile.
- **What may be published.** The results are internal. Copy may describe the *method* on „Wie bewertet die KI?",
  but may state no accuracy figure until agreement with human raters is measured (memo 13 impl. 10).

### 9.5 The human-style DaF review rubric (run by reviewer agents)

**Two reviewers per unit, with different personas (design):**

- **R1 „Prüferin"**, a licensed Goethe/telc examiner: exam fidelity, rubric application, task clarity.
- **R2 „Kursleiterin DaF/DaZ"**: naturalness, level, didactics, scene plausibility.

In addition, **one level reviewer per level and round** reads all 12 units for coherence, spirals and persona.
After exit, a **fresh-eyes reviewer** audits a random 20 % of units to catch reviewer drift.

**The score sheet, 6 criteria × 5 points = 30.** It keeps A1.1's five dimensions (Deutsch, Niveau, Dialog,
Prüfung, Kohärenz, as used in `REVIEW-daf-23`) and splits item quality out as its own criterion, because it was
the largest finding class.

| Criterion | 5 | 3 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D — Deutsch** (correctness, idiom, register) | no errors; idiomatic; register fits each relationship | isolated unidiomatic phrasing, nothing wrong on a model surface | an error in a key, a model text or a rule card |
| **N — Niveau** | lexis, grammar and length exactly on level; progression felt | isolated look-aheads, glossed | systematically too hard or too easy |
| **H — Handlung** (situation, can-dos, input) | a believable scene in which the can-dos are actually performed | constructed but functional | a can-do that is only claimed |
| **A — Aufgaben** (items, feedback) | unambiguous; the answer follows from the German prompt; plausible distractors; explanations name the real error | some weak distractors | ambiguous, or solvable without German |
| **P — Prüfung** | Teil format, sizes, plays, rubric profile correct | small deviation, flagged `scaffolded` | a wrong format that trains the wrong behaviour |
| **K — Kohärenz** | cast, facts, progression and spirals consistent | small breaks | contradictions |

**Severity.**

- **BLOCKER:** a false fact, wrong German in a key or model surface, an answer that cannot be derived, a legal
  breach, or a misrepresented exam format.
- **MAJOR:** unnatural German on a model surface, a level breach, a can-do not performed, an ambiguous item, a
  persona contradiction, or a gate hole that lets a class through.
- **MINOR:** style, gloss wording, or a distractor that could be better.

**Exit criteria per unit:** 0 BLOCKER, 0 MAJOR, every criterion ≥ 4, and ≤ 5 logged MINOR. **Per level:** a
mean of ≥ 25/30. For comparison, A1.1 closed at a mean of 21.6/25, i.e. 86 % (`REVIEW-daf-23`).

**Findings are structured JSON, and classes turn into rules.**

- Each finding has the shape `{unitId, path, quote, problem, fix, severity, scope: "instance" | "class", proposedRule}`.
- Every `class` finding goes to a **rule-smith** agent. It writes the gate rule, a failing fixture, and a test
  **over all registered courses**, then re-validates every unit. This is CLAUDE.md's "close a finding class with a
  rule + a test".
- If a class recurs in ≥ 3 units of a level during P4, that level's waves stop until the rule exists
  (stop-the-line).

### 9.6 What the machines cannot check (the reviewers' brief)

The reviewers' prompt lists exactly what G0–G13 cannot see, so their attention goes there:

- whether a turn is something a real person would say in that situation (pragmatics);
- whether a collocation is natural even though it is grammatical (*eine Entscheidung treffen*,
  not ✗ *eine Entscheidung machen*);
- cultural accuracy and stereotypes;
- whether a rule card's simplification has become false;
- whether a distractor is defensible as correct;
- tone and humour;
- whether a scene is boring. Blandness is the characteristic failure of templated production and gets its own
  line in criterion H.

## 10. Production plan with agent counts

### 10.1 Operating rules for every agent (design)

1. **Agents never touch the database.** All database facts come from the snapshot. The lemma → `words.id` match
   and any audio URLs are resolved by the orchestrator with one SQL query at integration (override 2026-09-27; db
   snapshot header).
2. **Definition of done for an author run:** the unit validates with exit 0 on G0–G7, G9, G10 and G12. The agent
   runs the validator itself before returning. A run that returns a red unit is a failed run, not a draft.
3. **Every author prompt carries the same five items:**
   - the unit spec;
   - the level profile;
   - the cast bible;
   - one **exemplar unit** from the pilot, at the same level;
   - the list of gates, including the reviewers' "what machines can't check" (§9.6).
4. **Roles are split so each agent owns a small surface (design):**
   - **S „Szene & Text"**: Start, the three LS inputs, LS4 texts, audio line script.
   - **I „Items"**: structured input, practice pools, exit items, can-do proofs, explanations.
   - **T „Prüfungsaufgaben"**: LS4 items against the Teil template, the two *Aufgabe* tasks, checklists, model
     text, speaking moves.
   - Items depend on texts, so S runs first; I and T then run in parallel.
5. **Generators are code, not agents.** They produce lexicon drills, dictations from input lines, minimal-pair
   perception items and review cards. What the code produces cannot drift.
6. **Every finding class becomes a rule** (§9.5). Rules apply to **all** units at once.

### 10.3 The per-unit pipeline

```
spec (frozen) ─► S: texts + audio script ─► gates G3–G6, G9, G12 on texts ─┐

                                                                           └─► T: exam blocks + Aufgabe ─┤
generators (code): drills, dictations, perception, cards ────────────────────────────────┤

                                                                                         │
                               instance findings ─► fixer ─► gates ─► re-review (only failed criteria)
                               class findings    ─► rule-smith ─► rule + test (all courses) ─► re-validate all
```

### 10.4 Go / no-go after the pilot (design thresholds)

The pilot passes if all six hold:

- All 8 pilot units reach 0 BLOCKER / 0 MAJOR in ≤ 4 review rounds. A1.1 needed 23.
- Blind-solver disagreement on first submission is ≤ 5 % of items.
- LanguageTool/Hunspell findings on first submission are ≤ 1 per 1,000 words.
- The reviewers' mean is ≥ 25/30, with no criterion below 4.
- Measured agent-minutes per unit are within 2 × the estimate.
- G13 passes for the profiles the pilots use.

If any threshold fails, the rails get fixed before P4: templates, exemplars and rules. More authors are never
added to compensate.
